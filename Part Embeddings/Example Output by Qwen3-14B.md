Under German law (§ 574 BGB), a landlord **cannot unilaterally revoke a valid termination notice** once it has been properly issued. ### **1. Termination Based on Invalid Reasons**
If the landlord issued the termination for **unlawful reasons** (e.g., discrimination, retaliation, or failure to meet legal requirements for termination), the tenant may challenge the termination in court. If the court rules the termination invalid, the landlord must **retract it**, and the lease continues.

---

### **2. Hardship for the Tenant (§ 574 BGB)**
A termination may be **revoked** if:
- **(1)** The tenant or their family faces **unreasonable hardship** (e.g., medical needs, inability to relocate), and this hardship is **not justified by the landlord's interests**.
- **(2)** No **reasonable alternative housing** (Ersatzwohnraum) exists for the tenant.
- **(3)** The termination notice cites **invalid reasons** (e.g., the landlord’s stated interest in "Eigenbedarf" is not credible).
- **(4)** The termination notice **violates the lease agreement** (e.g., by worsening the tenant’s position).

In such cases, the **tenant may request revocation** through a court or mediation, and the landlord may be legally obligated to withdraw the termination.

---

### **3. Landlord’s Own Mistake or Change of Circumstances**
If the landlord **mistakenly issued the termination** (e.g., due to incorrect information about their own needs), they might **unilaterally revoke it** if the tenant agrees. However, this is not guaranteed and depends on mutual consent.

---

### **Key Legal Notes**
- **§ 574 BGB** governs termination rights for landlords, but it does **not grant the landlord the right to revoke a valid termination** after it has been properly issued.

- **Hardship claims** require documentation (e.g., medical reports, financial statements).

### **Conclusion**
A landlord **cannot arbitrarily revoke a termination notice**. However, the termination may be **invalid** or **retracted** if:
- The reasons for termination are **unlawful**,

In Germany, a landlord **can** revoke (widerrufen) a notice of termination (Kündigung) under certain conditions, but this is not automatic and depends on specific legal criteria laid out in the German Civil Code (Bürgerliches Gesetzbuch – BGB), particularly in **§ 574 BGB**.

### Can the landlord cancel a termination notice?  
Yes, but **only under certain conditions**, and **only if the tenant successfully argues that the termination is unjustified or unlawful**. The landlord is not allowed to simply cancel a termination notice once it has been issued, unless specific legal grounds apply.

---

### When can a landlord revoke a termination notice?

A landlord may be **obliged to revoke** a termination notice if the following conditions are met (as outlined in the law and explained in your text):

#### 1. **Unreasonable Hardship (Unzumutbare Härte) for the tenant**
- If the termination would cause **unreasonable hardship** to the tenant or their family (e.g., financial problems, health issues, or lack of alternative housing), the landlord may be required to revoke the termination.
- This hardship must not be covered by the landlord's legitimate interests (e.g., the landlord’s need for the property).

#### 2. **No Suitable Replacement Housing (Kein zumutbarer Ersatzwohnraum)**
- If there is **no suitable replacement housing** available for the tenant (e.g., no other apartment in the area, or the only available options are too expensive or unsuitable), the termination may be revoked.

#### 3. **Invalid or Unjustified Reasons for Termination**
- If the **reasons stated in the termination notice** (e.g., self-occupation, renovation, etc.) are **not valid** or do not correspond to a **lawful interest** of the landlord, the termination may be revoked.
- For example, if the landlord is not genuinely in need of the property for self-occupation, or if the renovation claim is not credible, the termination may be considered invalid.

#### 4. **The Termination Agreement is Unfair to the Tenant**
- If the termination is based on a **clause or agreement that is unfair or disadvantageous to the tenant**, the tenant may be able to challenge the termination and have it revoked.

---

### Important Notes:
- The tenant must **challenge the termination** in court or through mediation (e.g., with a housing authority or mediation service).
- The landlord **cannot unilaterally revoke** the termination notice unless the above conditions are met and a court or authority rules in favor of the tenant.
- The landlord is only required to revoke the notice if the termination was **unlawful or unjustified**.
